Calinos Prime

Calinos is a partially terraformed world caught between what it was and what its people are forcing it to become.

The surface is divided roughly into three zones that exist in constant tension with one another.

The cities sit on the most geologically stable ground available — wide, low-built, heavily engineered urban centers surrounded by walls and firebreaks. Not hive spires. Dense, practical, built to survive ground instability and environmental assault rather than to impress. Each major city serves as the administrative and industrial heart of its district. Underground infrastructure — water pipes, geothermal taps, transit tunnels, forge complexes — extends as deep beneath them as the buildings rise above.

The Reclaimed Zones stretch between cities — maintained corridors of workable land wrested from the ashlands through generations of effort. Agricultural areas, filtered air zones, managed green spaces. They exist only because people maintain them continuously. Let the maintenance lapse and the ashlands reclaim them within a generation.

The Ashlands fill everything else — vast grey wastelands of cooled volcanic rock and industrial fallout that separate everything from everything. Not dead ground. Active, shifting, dangerous. Ash dunes move like desert sand. Toxic geysers vent without warning. Things live out there that adapted to conditions nothing should survive in. During a Searing the ashlands expand, pressing against the reclaimed zones and city perimeters simultaneously.

Beneath all of it is a fourth zone — the deep geology. Mineral rich, geothermally violent, the source of both Caligno's industrial value and its instability. Profundex sits closest to it. Everyone lives above consequences they cannot fully predict.

Calinos Culture

The Volunteers

All districts on Calinos originally consisted of all-volunteer civil service and defense personnel, especially rooted in fire response and first-responder traditions due to the unstable nature of the planet. As the population grew, volunteering became unsustainable to keep up with the expanding civil and defense activities, so paid professional civil work began to supplement the volunteers.

Volunteering remains the only resource in some areas of the planet, and in regions where it is not, it is still seen as a great duty in addition to normal work. It is almost expected that each Calinosian volunteers their time in some way to Calinos Prime, though there are no legal edicts mandating service. Calinos society views people who do not volunteer as "Anchors"—a derogatory term implying someone is a weight upon society rather than a benefit.

The Advisory Tradition

Before formal militarization, the Calinos Ashwalkers developed a renowned tradition of sending advisor teams to other industrial and hive worlds. These teams taught civil fire response, emergency medical, hazmat protocols, and structural rescue techniques to worlds with similar environmental challenges.

This tradition led to the informal shorthand across the Imperium of referring to any Calinosian relief or advisory worker as "Volunteers"—a designation that would later become entrenched in formal regimental nomenclature. It also contributed to the organic civilian veneration phenomenon known as the Ash Watch across worlds the Custodians helped.
